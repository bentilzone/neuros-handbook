How a seller puts a product on sale, decides who sees it, and how Neuros works out each buyer's price.

## 1. Create an offer
Under **Offers → New offer**, pick the catalogue product and set your terms:
- your SKU, standard price and unit;
- quantity breaks;
- [visibility](../01-glossary/30-selling-and-pricing.mdx#visibility);
- [supply](../01-glossary/30-selling-and-pricing.mdx#supply-stock-mode) and lead time;
- warranty, minimum order and order steps;
- whether buyers see your quantity;
- where you deliver.

Prices start in your company's currency. The offer is a **draft** until you publish it.

## 2. Publish
Publishing puts it in the marketplace for the buyers its visibility allows, within seconds. Published offers count towards your plan's limit. **Withdraw** takes it off sale and keeps its history.

## 3. Give customers their prices
Under **Pricing**:
- **Price lists:** create one (name, currency, dates), add offers with their own quantity breaks, then attach it to customers' accounts in their terms.
- **Contract prices:** a negotiated price for one customer on one offer, with a minimum quantity, your reference and dates. End it when it no longer applies.
- **Promotions:** percent off, amount off or a fixed price, on chosen offers, for every buyer or approved customers only, between dates. Pause, resume or end it.

## 4. How the price is decided
Neuros uses one rule everywhere, the [pricing precedence](../01-glossary/30-selling-and-pricing.mdx#pricing-precedence):
1. the customer's contract price;
2. else their price list, at the highest quantity break reached;
3. else your standard price or the offer's own break.

The best promotion then applies to 2 or 3, never to a contract. Prices convert between units through the product's conversions and are rounded to the currency's precision.

The **price check** on Offers and Pricing shows exactly what a chosen customer would pay for a quantity, step by step. It is the same calculation buyers get.

## 5. Quote a customer
For a large or unusual deal, send one customer a **quotation**: fixed prices (theirs, or your own per line), freight, lead time and terms, valid until a date. Customers can also send you a **request for quotation** from your offer. Revise a quotation to change it; the customer accepts or declines the latest revision. See Quotes in the [distributor guide](../11-distributor/55-quotes.mdx).

## 6. Take orders
Customers place **orders** at their price, or at a quotation's. You accept or reject each one, or accept automatically. Accepting fixes the prices, claims credit for an order on credit, and reserves the stock; what is short is backordered until it arrives. Either side can propose a change before it ships. See Orders in the [distributor guide](../11-distributor/57-orders.mdx). The whole flow on both sides, with a video: [Quote to cash](./65-quote-to-cash.mdx).

## 7. Ship
Under **Fulfilment**, start a shipment from an accepted order's reserved stock, pick it from the bins on the pick list, pack it, and dispatch it. The stock leaves inventory at cost as it goes. Record delivery with a photo or the signed note; the customer can confirm they received it. See Fulfilment in the [distributor guide](../11-distributor/85-fulfilment.mdx).

## 8. Invoice and get paid
Each shipment is invoiced as it is dispatched (or the whole order on acceptance, by your setting), and the customer's billing contact is emailed it. Customers pay by the arrangement on their account: you confirm transfers when the money arrives, prepay orders are reserved once paid, credit comes back as invoices are paid, and escrow is released by the customer's instruction. Every invoice and payment posts to your books on its own. See Invoices in the [distributor guide](../11-distributor/58-invoices.mdx).

## Emails
The other party is emailed when something needs them:

| When | Who is emailed |
|---|---|
| You send a quotation | The person at the customer who asked for it (else their administrator) |
| A customer submits an order | Your company administrator |
| You accept or reject an order | The person who placed it |
| You dispatch a shipment | The person who placed the order, with the tracking reference |
| An invoice is issued | The customer's billing contact |
| You confirm a payment | The person who placed the order |

## What the buyer sees
- A public offer shows your standard price to everyone.
- Your approved customers see *their* price, with its source (*Your price list*, *Your contract price*).
- Nobody sees your other customers' prices, your lists' contents or your costs.

## Live
Offers, price lists, contracts and promotions update on every colleague's screen as they change, and in the marketplace for buyers.
