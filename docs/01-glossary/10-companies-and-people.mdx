### Company (organisation)
A business on Neuros: a supplier, a distributor or a reseller. All of them are peers. A company has a legal name, a country, a functional currency, a timezone and the capabilities Neuros approved for it. In the engine and some screens it is called an *organisation*; the handbook uses *company*.

### Capability
What Neuros has verified a company may do. Capabilities are granted by the platform operator when it approves the company's application, never bought with a plan.
- **Supply:** sells and holds stock, never buys.
- **Distribution:** buys, holds stock and sells on.
- **Purchasing:** buys only.

Capabilities decide which parts of the app a company sees. A purchasing-only company has no Selling or Inventory pages, and Neuros refuses those actions even if someone tries them directly.

### Functional currency
The currency a company keeps its books in. It comes from the company's country (Ghana → cedis, Nigeria → naira) and is fixed once journals are posted or stock is valued. Every price, cost and journal the company records starts in it. Another currency is always a deliberate choice in the field.

### Company timezone
The timezone the company's dates and accounting periods follow. It defaults to the country's zone, so a sale at 23:30 UTC on 31 October belongs to November for a Lagos company (UTC+1). It is fixed once journals are posted.

### Person (user)
Someone who signs in. One person has one sign-in (email and password, optionally two-step verification) and can belong to several companies.

### Member (membership)
A person's place in one company: their roles, their spending and approval limits, and whether they are active or suspended. A person who works for two companies has two memberships.

### Invitation
How a company adds a member. The invitee gets an email link, accepts it, and becomes a member with the roles the invitation named. It can be revoked before it is accepted, and it expires if not used.

### Role
A named set of permissions given to members: *Company administrator*, *Accountant*, *Storekeeper* and so on. Neuros provides standard roles, and a company can create its own. Nobody can grant a permission they don't hold themselves.

### Permission
One thing a member may do, written as *area:action*: `stock:receive`, `journal:post`, `coa:manage`. Reading is always a separate permission from changing, so a view-only role is possible. The handbook shows the permission each page needs.

### System administrator
The person who set the company up on Neuros. They hold every permission, and the company cannot remove, suspend or demote them; only the platform operator can hand the role to someone else.

### Two-step verification (MFA)
A six-digit code from an authenticator app, asked for at sign-in after the password. Sensitive actions require a session that signed in with it: granting roles, posting and reversing journals, closing periods, changing posting rules and revaluing foreign currency. Recovery codes, shown once, let you in if you lose the device.

### Segregation of duties
A company setting, on by default: the person who prepared something cannot approve it. It applies to manual journals, stock adjustments and stock counts. Someone else must approve or post.

### Company switcher
The control at the top left that moves you between the companies you belong to. Switching signs you into the other company properly: you see only what that membership allows.

### API client
A machine login for a company's own systems (an ERP, a shop) to use Neuros's API. It has scopes like a role. Its secret is shown once when it is created or rotated, and never again.

### Audit trail
The record of who changed what, when, and why. Every change in Neuros is written to it at the same moment as the change itself, so nothing changes without a trace. Each record's history appears on its own page; the full log is under **Audit log**.

### Live updates
Pages that others change update on their own: a colleague's approval, a customer's request or a supplier's new price appears without reloading. The **Live** dot at the top right shows the connection. When it reconnects, open pages refresh to catch up.
