### Catalogue
The one shared list of real products on Neuros. Every company sells from it, so the same pump is one product however many sellers offer it. That is what lets a buyer compare sellers.

### Product
One real item in the catalogue: its name, brand, part number (MPN), barcode (GTIN), category, attributes and units. A product belongs to no company. Prices and stock belong to the sellers' offers and warehouses.

### Product reference
Neuros's own identifier for a product, shown as `NEU-PROD-000001`. It never changes, even if the product's name is corrected.

### Category
Where a product sits in the tree, for example *Pumps › Borehole pumps*. A category defines the attributes its products must describe (power, voltage, size…), and sub-categories inherit them.

### Proposed category
A category a company has suggested and the platform has not yet approved. The company that proposed it can rename it, add sub-categories under it or withdraw it. Others don't see it until it is approved.

### Attribute
A described property of a product, defined by its category: a number with a unit, a choice from a list, yes/no or text. The marketplace's filters are built from attributes, so only the category's own attributes are accepted.

### Unit and unit conversion
The base unit a product is counted in (each, metre, pair) and the other units it is sold in, such as *1 carton = 4 each*. Prices convert through these: a carton costs four times the each price.

### Contributing a product
Adding a product the catalogue doesn't have yet. It starts as your draft, goes to the platform for review when you submit it, and becomes visible to everyone only once published. A barcode already in the catalogue is refused and points you to the existing product.

### Product review
The platform's check of a contributed product. The reviewer can publish it, send it back for changes with a note, or later retire it.

### Tracking (lot, serial)
Whether each unit of a product is followed individually. *Serial*: every unit has its own number. *Lot*: units come in batches with a lot number and often an expiry date. *None*: units are just counted. It follows the product type and decides what you enter when stock is received.
