### Account (trading relationship)
The standing arrangement between a seller and a buyer: the buyer's account with that seller. It carries the terms they trade on. Both sides see it: the seller under **Customers**, the buyer under **Suppliers**.

### Account status
- **Waiting for approval:** the buyer asked (or the seller invited) and the seller hasn't answered.
- **Approved:** they trade on the agreed terms.
- **Declined:** the seller said no.
- **Suspended:** the seller paused trading, with a reason; it can be reinstated.
- **Ended:** closed for good. Either side may end it.

### Terms
What an approved account trades on, set by the seller:
- the price list the buyer gets;
- the payment modes allowed and the default one, with payment days;
- whether the buyer may buy on credit, and up to how much;
- the largest single order;
- the dates the terms apply between;
- the seller's private notes about the customer.

### Credit limit
The most a credit-eligible buyer may owe a seller at once, in the seller's own currency. The seller sets it, optionally with an expiry date.

### Credit in use (exposure)
The part of the limit already claimed: orders on credit that the seller accepted and the buyer has not yet paid. Each order claims its value when it is accepted and gives it back as its invoices are paid, or all at once when it is cancelled. **Available** is the limit less what is in use.

### Credit hold
A pause the seller puts on an account, with a reason: no new orders on credit until it is lifted. Open orders and invoices are untouched.

### Credit override
A one-off approval to go over a buyer's limit by a set amount, with a reason and an expiry. The next order that needs it uses it, once. Only the seller sees overrides.

### Payment mode
How a buyer may pay on an account: pay before dispatch, bank transfer, on credit, deposit with the balance later, milestones, or escrow.

### Payment notice
A buyer telling the seller an order is paid, with the bank reference and optionally the slip. It is not a payment until the seller confirms the money arrived; the seller can also say it did not, with a reason.

### Prepay
Paying before the order is fulfilled. Stock is reserved once the order is fully paid; one left unpaid past the seller's limit cancels itself.

### Escrow
A regulated partner holds the buyer's money for an order. The seller ships once the partner confirms the funds; they are released to the seller by the buyer's own instruction, or a set number of days after delivery, never by delivery alone. A dispute holds them. Before release, the seller can refund them.

### Escrow agreement
The record of one order's escrow (*ESC-2026-000001*): the amount held, in the order's currency, what releases it (the buyer's instruction, or a number of days after delivery), any hold, and every instruction sent to the partner with its answer. Both sides see it in the order's Payment section.

### Supplier code
The buyer's own reference for a seller, such as their supplier number in their own system. Only the buyer sees it.

### External supplier
A supplier you buy from outside Neuros, kept as your own record under Suppliers, External: its codes and names, the currency it sells in, its payment terms and lead time. Only your company sees it. A seller on Neuros is reached through your account with them instead, never copied.

### Supplier bank details
Where an external supplier is paid. They change only by someone who may manage bank accounts, after two-step verification and with a reason; others see the last four digits of the account number.

### Seller directory
The list of selling companies a buyer can ask for an account with, under **Suppliers → Find sellers**.
