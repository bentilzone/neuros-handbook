### Offer
A seller's terms for one catalogue product: their SKU, standard price and unit, quantity breaks, who may see it, how it is supplied, lead time, warranty and where they deliver. An offer is the seller's private record; buyers see the listing made from it.

### Offer status
- **Draft:** being prepared, invisible to buyers.
- **Published:** live; buyers it is visible to can find it.
- **Withdrawn:** taken off sale, kept for history.

### Visibility
Who can find a published offer.
- **Everyone:** any buyer on Neuros finds it, with your standard price shown.
- **Approved customers:** only buyers you have an approved account with find it, and each sees their own price.
- **Invited buyers only:** only the approved customers you name.

### Supply (stock mode)
How an offer is fulfilled.
- **From stock:** sold from stock you hold; orders reserve it.
- **Special order:** ordered in for each sale, with your lead time.
- **Backorder allowed:** sold beyond what you hold; the rest follows.

### Standard price
The offer's own price, before any price list, contract or promotion. Public offers show it to everyone.

### Quantity break (tier)
A lower unit price from a minimum quantity, for example *₦276,000 from 5*. The highest break the ordered quantity reaches applies.

### Price list
A set of prices you give a group of customers, like *Gold resellers*. It lists offers with their own quantity breaks. You attach a price list to a customer's account in its terms, and that customer then sees those prices. The customer sees the list's name on their account, never your other lists.

### Contract price
A negotiated price for one customer on one offer, for a period, often with a minimum quantity and your contract reference. It beats the price list and the standard price, and promotions do not reduce it further.

### Promotion
A temporary reduction: a percentage off, an amount off or a fixed price, on chosen offers, for every buyer or approved customers only, between two dates. It can be paused, resumed and ended. If several apply, the single best one is used, plus any marked as stackable.

### Pricing precedence
The one rule Neuros uses to decide a buyer's price, in this order:
1. A quotation price: the seller's own price on a line of a quotation.
2. The buyer's contract price.
3. The buyer's price list, at the highest quantity break reached.
4. The offer's standard price or its own quantity break.

Promotions then apply to 3 or 4, never to a contract price. The result is rounded to the currency's precision.

### Price check
A tool on the seller's Offers and Pricing pages that answers "what would this customer pay for this quantity?". It shows each step of the precedence and why it applied, exactly as Neuros will compute it.

### Request for quotation
A buyer asking one seller to price some of the seller's offers, in given quantities, with when and where they are needed. Numbered from the buyer's own series (*RFQ-2026-000001*). The buyer needs an approved account with the seller and can only ask about offers it can see.

### Quotation
A seller's fixed offer of prices and terms to one buyer, valid until a date. Each line is priced like the price check, unless the seller sets their own price on it, and taxed with the offer's tax code. Once sent, its prices never change. Numbered from the seller's series (*Q-2026-000004*). The buyer can accept or decline it; an accepted quotation becomes an order.

### Revision
A new version of a quotation, with the same number (*Q-2026-000004 rev 2*). Sending it replaces the revision sent before it, which can no longer be accepted. Only the latest revision sent, still within its validity, can be accepted.

### Quote validity
How long a quotation can be accepted: the date the seller sets, or by default the company's quote validity days (14) from when it is sent. After it the quotation is *Expired*, and the seller can revise it.

### Order
One buyer buying from one seller, with a snapshot of what was agreed: both companies' names, tax ids and addresses, each product, its price and where the price came from, tax, freight, currency and how it is paid. Numbered from the seller's series (*SO-2026-000001*). The buyer drafts and submits it; the seller accepts or rejects it. Prices never change after it is priced, except through an approved amendment.

### Order statuses
An order carries three statuses that move separately, so one never implies another:
- **Order:** draft, waiting for approval, waiting for the seller, accepted, rejected, cancelled, completed.
- **Payment:** awaiting payment, part paid, paid, refunded.
- **Stock:** not reserved, partly reserved, stock reserved, then dispatch and delivery.

### Purchase approval
An order over the submitter's purchase limit waits for someone else with approval rights, within their own approval limit, before it goes to the seller. No purchase limit set means no limit.

### Automatic acceptance
The seller's choice to accept orders as soon as they are submitted: for every customer (Order acceptance in company settings) or for one (Accept their orders automatically, in that customer's terms). The credit and stock checks still apply; an order that fails them waits for a person.

### Backorder
The part of an accepted order that could not be reserved because stock was short. It waits until stock arrives and the seller reserves it. What happens when stock is short is the seller's **When stock is short** setting: backorder the rest, hold the line until it can be reserved whole, or refuse to accept.

### Amendment
A change to a waiting or accepted order (quantities, removed lines, a new delivery date) proposed by one side and approved or rejected by the other. Quantities keep their agreed price. An approved amendment makes a new revision of the order (*SO-2026-000004 rev 2*).
