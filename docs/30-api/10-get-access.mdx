An API client is a set of credentials that belongs to your company, not to a person. It keeps working when people come and go, and it can do exactly what its scopes allow, nothing more.

## Who can create one

A member with the `api_client:manage` permission, usually a company administrator, in a session signed in with two-step verification. Your plan sets how many clients your company can hold: see **Settings → Billing**.

## Create the client

1. In the app, open **API clients** and press **New client**.
2. Name it after the system that will use it, for example "ERP sync".
3. Choose its **scopes**: the permissions it may use. You can grant only permissions you hold yourself.
4. Press **Create**. Neuros shows the **client ID** and **client secret** once.
5. Store the secret in your system's secret manager, then press **I have saved it**.

The [API clients page of the distributor guide](../11-distributor/120-api-clients.mdx) walks the screens.

## Choose scopes

Scopes are the same permissions people get through roles, for example `offer:read`, `offer:manage`, `stock:read`. The [reference](./reference/index.mdx) shows the scope each operation needs. A few combinations:

| The system | Scopes |
|---|---|
| Reads stock for a shop front | `stock:read`, `warehouse:read` |
| Publishes offers from an ERP | `catalogue:read`, `offer:read`, `offer:manage`, `offer:publish` |
| Keeps customer prices in step | `pricelist:read`, `pricelist:manage`, `relationship:read` |
| Receives goods from a warehouse system | `stock:receive`, `warehouse:read` |
| Reads the books for reporting | `coa:read`, `journal:read`, `report:financial` |

Grant the least that works. A leaked secret can do exactly what its scopes allow. A token can also ask for fewer scopes than its client holds: see [Authentication](./40-authentication.mdx#narrow-a-token).

## Look after the secret

- **Never** put it in a browser, a mobile app or a repository. It belongs on a server.
- **Rotate** it from the API clients page if it may have leaked. The old secret stops working at once.
- **Revoke** a client you no longer use. Every call with it fails from then on, and this cannot be undone.
- Every call a client makes is in your company's **audit log**, under API requests.
